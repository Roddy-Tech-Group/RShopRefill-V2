01 Pipeline

How a single decision is made

Every order and every wallet funding calls one function, RiskGate::guard(), before any money moves. The gate assembles a context (who, how much, from which device/IP, which categories), runs it through the signals, and keeps two scores: the total (every fired signal) and the abuse subscore (abuse-family signals only). The total can raise a step-up; only the abuse subscore can reach review or deny. Both bucket through the same bands, the most severe outcome wins, and an immutable record is written. Overrides can force a decline regardless of score.

RiskContext→ 23 signals→ total + abuse scores→ bands→ outcome→ persist + act

Each signal is a small, self-contained rule that reads one data source and either fires (adding its configured weight) or stays silent. Nothing is a black box: the exact signals that fired, their weights, and the policy version are all stored, so any decision can be reproduced and explained months later — to you, to an auditor, or as chargeback dispute evidence.

Design rule. Weights, band cut-offs, the band→outcome map, and every threshold live in config/risk.php, not in code. Tuning the engine is a config change, never a deploy of new logic.

02 Switches

The two switches that govern everything

The engine has exactly two feature flags, both seeded OFF. They are independent, and together they define four states.

Master risk_engine_enabledEnforce risk_engine_enforce_enabledWhat happens
OFF—Fully inert. assess() returns before touching any risk table. Checkout & funding behave exactly as before. ← current production state.
ONOFFShadow Scores, records an assessment, logs the would-be outcome — but always returns allow. Zero customer impact. This is how you collect data.
ONONLive The decision is acted on: step-up, review-hold, or decline.
Golden path. Master ON + Enforce OFF first. Watch the shadow data for weeks. Only then flip enforcement, and narrowly. The flags are in Admin → Settings (keys features.risk_engine_enabled and features.risk_engine_enforce_enabled).

03 Outcomes

The four things a decision can do

Modelled on the card-scheme / PSD2 mental model of frictionless → challenge → decline. Severity orders them allow < step_up < review < deny; when more than one rule applies, the most severe wins.

allow

Proceed normally. The default for a clean, low-risk action.

step_up

Force identity verification (Smile ID) before continuing. The customer can self-clear, then retry. Reuses the KYC flow.

review

Hold the attempt and queue it for a human. Neutral "under review" message to the customer; nothing is charged.

deny

Refuse with a customer-safe message. Reason codes are recorded internally, never shown.

04 Scoring

Two-track scoring: verify the new, punish the abusive

Signals belong to one of two families, and the engine keeps a score for each track:

  • Identity / context family (new account, unverified state, high value, gift-card category, untrusted device): raises the total score, which is capped at step-up. Circumstantial evidence can only ever ask the customer to verify their identity, something a legitimate customer self-clears in minutes and a fraudster with a stolen card cannot.
  • Abuse family (declines, velocity bursts, multi-account linkage, bot failures, chargeback history): forms the abuse subscore, the only score that can reach review or deny. Punishment requires evidence of abusive behaviour, never mere newness.

Both scores bucket through the same bands; the most severe outcome wins; overrides can still force a decline. The philosophy: the engine doesn't ask "does this customer have risk signals?", it asks "is this behaviour meaningfully inconsistent with legitimate use?"

BandScore ≥Total scoreAbuse subscore
CRITICAL80step_up (capped)deny
HIGH55step_up (capped)review
MEDIUM30step_up—
LOW0allow—

Worked examples

First-timer, own device, $100 eSIM: total 11 → allow. First-timer, $600 gift card, no KYC: total 77, abuse 0 → step-up, verifies and proceeds.

Card tester (declines + device velocity + bot failures): abuse 62 → review; with a shared device, 87 → deny. Repeat chargeback offender: abuse 55 → review.

A moderate abuse subscore also lifts the total past 30, so a suspected multi-accounter faces an identity challenge before review territory. Friction lands exactly where evidence exists.

06 Overrides

Two signals that force a decline outright

Some states shouldn't depend on a score crossing a cut-off. When an override signal fires, its outcome is forced regardless of the band. Both currently force deny.

SignalForcesFires when
CONFIRMED_FRAUD_LABELdenyAccount carries a confirmed_fraud label within 365d
ACCOUNT_BANNEDdenyAccount is banned
Reserved slot. SANCTIONS_HIT → deny is wired in the override map but commented out; it activates the day AML/sanctions screening is added (recommended as a one-time check bundled into Tier-2 KYC at ~$0.35/check).

07 Placement

Where the gate sits, and what the customer sees

The gate runs at the two money entry points, before anything is written: CheckoutService::placeOrder and WalletFundingService::initializeFunding. Because it runs pre-write, a held or declined attempt costs zero rows. When enforcing, each outcome becomes an HTTP response the storefront already understands:

OutcomeHTTPBody flagCustomer experience
step_up422kyc_requiredSmile verification launches; retry after verifying
review422risk_review"Being reviewed, nothing to do" — neutral hold
deny403risk_declinedGeneric security decline; contact support
Fail-open by design. The engine never breaks a checkout. Any unexpected internal fault is logged and the action is allowed through. In shadow that's a no-op; even enforcing, availability wins over a scoring bug.

08 Reviews

The manual review queue & its lifecycle

A review outcome opens one queue entry (deduped per customer + context, so repeated taps never spawn duplicates) and holds the attempt. Because the gate is pre-write, "hold" means the attempt is declined-pending-review; an approval grants a clearance window so the customer's retry sails through, rather than half-creating an order that must be resumed.

StatusEffect on the customer
pendingHeld. Awaiting a decision.
escalatedStill held; flagged for a second/senior look.
approvedCleared. Retry is allowed for 24h (clearance window) without re-triggering.
rejectedStays refused; auto-records a confirmed_fraud label so future attempts hit the deny override.

Ownership & SLA

  • SLA target 12h. The queue badges anything older as breached; the daily report emails owners about breaches.
  • Owners come from config('risk.review.owners') — default dev@roddytechgroup.com. Set RISK_REVIEW_OWNERS (comma-separated) to add analysts without removing the standing overseer. Every owner is alerted when a review opens and on SLA breach.
  • Every approve / reject / escalate writes an audit-log row with the admin, before/after state, and reason.

Worked at Admin → Risk → Review queue. Open a row to see the score, the exact signals that fired with their detail, and the approve / reject / escalate actions.

09 KYC

Risk-driven identity step-up

The gate unifies risk and KYC. A step_up outcome forces identity verification (Smile ID) on the engine's own authority — a HIGH-risk action can demand verification even on a small amount, via a risk floor the engine hands the KYC tier service. KYC's ordinary value-based step-up (anything at/above the admin's kyc_tier2_threshold_usd, default $500) still applies independently.

Sequencing. Because a real step-up mints a live Smile session, only enable risk enforcement once the KYC/Smile program is live and sandbox-verified. Both kyc_enforce and the risk flags are off today, so nothing steps up in production yet.

10 Feedback

Labels: how the engine learns

Ground-truth labels close the loop and let the engine improve instead of ossifying. Four labels, recorded from Admin → Risk → Record a label or automatically:

chargeback

A dispute happened. Feeds CHARGEBACK_HISTORY (adds weight) and the dashboard's chargeback-rate metric.

confirmed_fraud

Proven fraud. Trips the deny override on every future attempt. Written automatically when a review is rejected.

false_positive

The engine was wrong. Recorded so precision can be measured as data accrues.

good

Known-good actor. Ground truth for tuning.

Distinction worth remembering: a single chargeback adds risk weight (it doesn't ban anyone), while confirmed fraud is a hard deny.

11 Records

What's stored, and for how long

TableHoldsMutable?
risk_assessmentsOne immutable row per decision: score, band, outcome, enforced flag, IP, device, amount, policy versionNo — append-only
risk_signalsEach fired signal for an assessment: code, weight, PII-safe detailNo
risk_reviewsThe queue: status, assignee, decision reason, clearance windowYes (status changes)
risk_labelsGround-truth feedback (chargeback / fraud / false-positive / good)Append-only
  • Every assessment is stamped with the policy version in force, so a decision is always reproducible against the exact weights that produced it.
  • No PANs, CVVs, OTPs, or secrets are ever stored in signal detail.
  • Retention 24 months, then risk:prune deletes old assessments (signals cascade). Reviews and labels are kept as audit trail and ground truth.

12 Dashboard

Reading the metrics

At Admin → Risk (7 / 30 / 90-day windows). Everything is computed from stored rows — no sampling, nothing silently truncated.

  • Review / decline / step-up rate — share of assessments landing on each outcome. Your friction and workload dials.
  • Shadow-vs-enforce delta — "would have acted on N of M". The single most important number before you enforce: it's exactly what enforcement would change, watched with zero customer impact.
  • Chargeback rate — chargeback labels ÷ paid orders, against the 0.5% acquirer ceiling. The survival metric for a gift-card merchant.
  • Band distribution & top-firing signals — where score is coming from; your tuning shortlist.
  • Queue health — pending, escalated, SLA-breached.

14 Rollout

The procedure for turning it on

  1. Enable scoring, keep enforcement off. Flip risk_engine_enabled → on. The engine begins recording assessments. Customers see nothing.
  2. Collect 2–4 weeks of shadow data. Let real traffic flow through it.
  3. Read the shadow delta & tune. On the dashboard, see what would have been reviewed/declined. Adjust weights, band cut-offs, and the band→outcome map in config/risk.php against reality. Bump the policy version when you do.
  4. Enforce the narrowest, highest-confidence rules first. Flip risk_engine_enforce_enabled → on with the map set so only the certain cases act (e.g. confirmed-fraud/banned overrides and extreme velocity), widening gradually.
  5. Watch chargeback rate & false-positive labels. Keep enforcement scoped and reversible via the flag at every step.

15 Runbook

Common tasks

Start collecting data

Admin → Settings → turn risk_engine_enabled on. Leave enforce off.

Work the queue

Admin → Risk → Review queue → open a row → Approve (clears 24h) / Reject (refuses + labels fraud) / Escalate.

Record a chargeback

Admin → Risk → Record a label → customer ID + chargeback. Feeds history & the rate metric.

Clear a false positive

Record a false_positive (or good) label; approve the held review to release the customer.

Change who owns the queue

Set RISK_REVIEW_OWNERS="dev@roddytechgroup.com,analyst@…". Additive — keep yours.

Re-tune a threshold

Edit config/risk.php (weight, cut-off, or map), bump RISK_POLICY_VERSION, ship config.

16 Roadmap

Deliberately left as typed slots

The explainable, auditable core is built; these seams are wired but dormant, to be filled when the data or need arrives — so "industry standard" stays a trajectory, not a one-time claim.

  • Phone OTP verification — when the flow ships, re-register PHONE_UNVERIFIED and the KYC phone tier becomes reachable.
  • AML / sanctions screening — SANCTIONS_HIT → deny override reserved; bundle a one-time check into Tier-2 KYC.
  • True geo distance/time — GEO_VELOCITY is dormant; re-enable on a real GeoIP distance/time provider instead of the country-change proxy.
  • Card-fingerprint reuse — PAYMENT_INSTRUMENT_REUSE activates once a gateway supplies fingerprints in metadata.
  • Automatic dispute webhooks — chargeback labels are recorded by hand today; per-gateway dispute webhooks can write them automatically (feeding History/Repeat without an admin touch).
  • Champion / challenger & ML — the policy-version stamp enables running a new weight set in shadow against the live one; the signals→score→policy structure is ML-ready when volume justifies it.
  • Guest checkout — off by policy: guests bypass the identity track entirely, so the engine could only hold or deny them, never clear them. Revisit only as a category-limited, low-cap flow.
